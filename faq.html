German Shepherd FAQ: quick, sourced answers

Short answers to the questions people ask most about German Shepherds, each linked to a reliable source and to our longer guides.

How long do German Shepherds live?

The American Kennel Club gives a life expectancy of 12 to 14 years[1]. A large study of UK veterinary records found a median lifespan of 10.3 years, with females living a little longer (11.1 years) than males (9.7 years)[2]. Good weight control, regular vet checks and buying from health-tested parents all help. See our lifespan section.

How much exercise does a German Shepherd need?

The Royal Kennel Club recommends more than two hours of exercise a day for an adult German Shepherd[3]. That should include mental work such as training and scent games, not just walking. Puppies need shorter, gentler activity while their joints develop. Read our exercise guide.

Do German Shepherds shed a lot?

Yes. Both the short and long coat varieties have a dense undercoat[4], and the Royal Kennel Club lists the breed as one that sheds and needs grooming more than once a week[3]. Expect heavier moults at certain times of year. Regular brushing with an undercoat rake helps.

Are German Shepherds good with children?

Many well-bred, well-socialised German Shepherds live happily with children, but they are large, strong dogs and some try to herd or nip running children. Always supervise dogs and children together, teach children to leave a resting dog alone, and choose a puppy from calm, confident parents or an adult rescue whose temperament is known.

Are German Shepherds aggressive?

German Shepherds are not inherently aggressive. The FCI breed standard describes the ideal temperament as well-balanced, self-assured and good-natured[4]. However, aggression was among the five most commonly recorded problems in German Shepherds in a UK veterinary study[2], so breeding, early socialisation and reward-based training matter. See our socialisation guide.

What is the difference between an Alsatian and a German Shepherd?

There is no difference. They are the same breed. In Britain the breed was renamed the Alsatian after the First World War and was registered as the German Shepherd Dog again from 1977[5]. Read the full history.

When was the German Shepherd breed created?

The breed was formalised in 1899, when Max von Stephanitz founded the Verein für Deutsche Schäferhunde (SV) and registered Horand von Grafrath as the first dog in its stud book[6].

How big do German Shepherds get?

The FCI breed standard gives a height of 60 to 65 cm and a weight of 30 to 40 kg for males, and 55 to 60 cm and 22 to 32 kg for females[4]. The American Kennel Club gives 24 to 26 inches and 65 to 90 lb for males, and 22 to 24 inches and 50 to 70 lb for females[1].

What health problems are common in German Shepherds?

The most commonly recorded problems in UK vet records were ear inflammation, osteoarthritis, diarrhoea, obesity and aggression[2]. The breed is also associated with hip and elbow dysplasia, degenerative myelopathy[7], exocrine pancreatic insufficiency[8] and bloat[9]. See our health guide.

What health tests should German Shepherd parents have?

The Royal Kennel Club recommends hip and elbow scoring under the BVA/KC schemes as a minimum, plus a DNA test for degenerative myelopathy and an eye test as best practice[3]. Ask to see the certificates for both parents.

How much should I feed a German Shepherd puppy?

Use the feeding guide on a complete large-breed puppy food, based on your puppy's age and expected adult weight, and weigh the food[10]. Split it into several small meals: up to four a day until about six months, then two[11]. Keep puppies lean rather than pushing fast growth[12]. See our puppy feeding guide.

When should a German Shepherd switch to adult food?

PDSA's vets suggest large and giant breed puppies usually stay on puppy food until around 18 months[13]. Ask your vet for advice on your individual dog.

Can a German Shepherd live in a flat?

It is possible but challenging. A Shepherd needs a lot of daily exercise and mental stimulation, and stairs can be hard on young or arthritic joints. If you live in a flat, you will need easy access to open space, plenty of time each day and a plan for when you are out.

Can German Shepherds be left alone?

Many Shepherds are strongly attached to their people and can find being alone difficult. Teach short, positive periods alone from puppyhood and build up gradually. No dog should be left alone for long stretches every day; use a dog walker or day care if you work away from home.

What is the best way to train a German Shepherd?

Use reward-based methods. The American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior recommends only reward-based training and advises against aversive tools such as shock collars, prong collars and choke chains[14]. Short, frequent sessions work best. See our training tips.

What is bloat and why does it matter for German Shepherds?

Bloat, or gastric dilatation and volvulus (GDV), is an emergency in which the stomach fills with gas and may twist. It is most common in large, deep-chested dogs[9]. Signs include retching without producing anything, a swollen belly, restlessness and drooling. Contact a vet immediately if you suspect it.
